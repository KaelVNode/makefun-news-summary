World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026

Two days of US-mediated talks between Ukraine and Russia ended in Abu Dhabi with agreement on a large prisoner-of-war exchange, but no ceasefire and no deal on territory. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had called the first day "constructive and quite substantive" (NPR, BBC).

World leaders opened the UN COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey. Secretary-General Antonio Guterres warned that the slow response to climate change had put the world "on a path to destruction" (AFP). Five nations backed a plan to double homes with rooftop solar by 2030.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said economic pressure on Iran was working well enough that "there's really no need for major combat operations any longer", while warning force remained an option if Tehran crossed "certain red lines" (New York Times). Iran designated Argentina's navy and air force "terrorist organizations", and the US charged three Iran-backed operatives over alleged plots in the US and Europe.

Israeli forces killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, local health officials said, as Israeli strikes also continued across southern Lebanon.

NATO scrambled jets after at least four drones strayed into Baltic states' airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia. Britain detained a US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia, and US prosecutors charged three men over an alleged plot targeting RAF Fairford airbase.

House Republicans blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Democrat Scott Colom won Mississippi's US Senate seat, defeating incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith, the Associated Press projected.

Brent crude rose $1.27 to settle at $92.76 a barrel (Dow Jones). Saudi Arabia said it intercepted seven Houthi missiles and drones, including cruise missiles aimed towards Riyadh. In Sudan, the army said it shot down drones as fighting hit the power grid, and Israel swore in its new Knesset.
